# Meal Preferences

---

## Who I am

22-year-old male, 1.94m, 85–90kg, training approximately 5 days per week.

**Daily targets:** 3,100–3,300 calories, at least 110g protein. Meals should also support
adequate iron intake and include a reasonable amount of fruit and vegetables across the week.

I will aim for about 1000 calories for lunch, and 1100 to 1300 calories for dinner. 1000-1500 calories each week
will come from snacks that are not part of the meal plan. Another 4000-4500 calories will come from eating out.
Therefor the meal plan should account for approximately 17000 to 17500 calories per week.

---

## What to plan each week

Plan **2 meal-prep meals per week plus 2 quick meals per week plus snacks.** Each meal prep produces a large batch that covers 
4 meals (eaten as lunches and dinners across several days). These are the
core of the weekly plan and the primary output of the meal planner.

Do not plan meals as breakfast — I handle that myself (see below).
Do not plan for eating-out meals — I eat out roughly 2 lunches and 2 dinners per week and
these do not need to appear in the plan.

---

## Breakfast (no planning needed)

I don't cook breakfast. I typically grab cashews or a protein bar on the way out, eat
something available at work, or skip it. Occasionally I make oatmeal. No breakfast planning
is needed — just ensure snacks that work for a grab-and-go morning are somewhere in the
weekly shop.

---

## Cooking style

**Minimal processing is a mild preference.** I avoid pre-processing ingredients most of the times — chopping vegetables, mincing garlic, grating cheese, slicing onions, and similar
tasks. I prefer alternatives that skip this step entirely. Examples:

- Baby carrots instead of chopping a large carrot
- Jarred minced garlic instead of fresh cloves
- Pre-washed bagged spinach or broccoli florets instead of a whole head
- Canned or frozen ingredients where they work just as well as fresh

**Few dishes.** I prefer meals that don't require many pots, pans, or utensils. One-pot or
sheet-pan meals are ideal where they fit.

I can tolerate exceptions to both of the above — I cook a variety of things and
don't follow these rules rigidly — but the above rules should generally be kept in mind.

---

## Meal prep meal examples

These are meals I have made before and enjoy. The meal planner can use these directly,
suggest variations, or use them as a reference point for the style and complexity of meal
I'm comfortable with.

**Chili**
Ground beef or pork, one can each of black beans, kidney beans, and diced tomatoes, frozen
corn, spices. Served with rice or pasta.

**Slow-cooked pork**
Pork half loin cooked in a slow cooker or low oven in soy sauce, Worcestershire sauce, and
water. Served with mushrooms and rice, topped with salsa.

**Chicken curry**
Chicken thighs with vegetables in a curry base — broth, coconut milk, or Japanese curry cubes
depending on what's available. Flexible on vegetables and base.

**Tacos / burritos**
Flour tortillas, canned salsa verde, rice (optional), seasoned ground beef or pork, or
leftover slow-cooked pork or chicken, frozen corn.

**Sausages**
Sausages cooked in a pan with broccoli, spinach, or similar. Served with bread and mustard.

**Pasta with tomato sauce**
Simple tomato sauce, meat served on the side.

**Sheet pan salmon and vegetables**
Salmon, potatoes, and vegetables roasted on a sheet pan.

## Simple meals (quick one-off cooks)

These are not meal-prepped. I make them when I need something fast — 15 minutes or less.
The planner can suggest one or two of these per week if it makes sense, but they are not
the main output.

- Frozen dumplings with broccoli or a similar vegetable
- Oatmeal and eggs, optionally with frozen fruit and peanut butter
- quesadillas

## Snacks

These don't need to be planned as meals but should appear in the weekly shop as needed.
Some snacks that I usually buy are listed below, but be sure to include snacks that are not
on this list, as I enjoy variety.

- Roasted unsalted cashews
- Barbeque rice crackers
- Corn thins / multigrain corn thins
- Sourdough bread with hummus (only good-quality sourdough)
- Yoghurt with frozen blueberries or frozen mango
- Frozen mango
- Bananas with peanut butter
- Trail mix (not too peanut- or raisin-heavy)
- Smoothies
- Cucumbers or pickles (must be crunchy)
- Oatmeal with fruit and/or peanut butter
